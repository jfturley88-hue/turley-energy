Today, PlanitBER is one file running in the assessor's own browser. It has no server, no
account and no network calls carrying homeowner data. The trial changes three things:
A government dataset is queried. The published BER record for a dwelling is pulled
from SEAI's National Administration System (NAS), by MPRN, on the homeowner's
consent.
Proof of ownership is sent to SEAI. Each request must carry evidence of ownership or
occupancy, such as a utility bill showing the MPRN (Criterion 3.4). SEAI keeps it for the
trial.
Modified BER files go to the DEAP engine. The agreed measures are sent as a
modified BER file to estimate the energy uplift of each package.
These involve personal data linked to a home and an identifiable household, matched
against a public body's register, for a new technology use. Under Article 35 of the GDPR
and the Data Protection Commission's guidance, that is enough to need a DPIA.
The processing
Everything below is processed for one purpose: producing a homeowner's cost plan,
pricing schedule and appendix. Fields marked new only arise in the trial. The homeowner,
and an agent where one acts for them, are the only data subjects. No special category
data, children's data, PPS numbers, financial details or photographs are collected.

SEAI gets aggregated and anonymised figures only: county, dwelling type, age band,
measures, estimate, grants and recorded outcome. It never gets the name, address,
Eircode, MPRN or BER number in a return. Its copies of the MPRN, proof of ownership and
request records come through the API itself, which SEAI holds as an independent
controller (clause 3.1).
Lawful basis, necessity and proportionality
The trial processing rests on consent. The core PlanitBER service stays on contract.
Necessity. The API does the same job as the PDF report the assessor already reads, with
less re-keying and fewer transcription errors. It takes no new categories of data about the
dwelling. The new items are the MPRN, proof of ownership and the consent record, and
SEAI makes each a condition of every request.
Minimisation. Requests are made one dwelling at a time, only after consent, and never in
bulk (Terms, clause 10). Before a utility bill is uploaded, everything except the name,
address and MPRN is blacked out: account number, balance, usage and payment details.
The homeowner's name never reaches a contractor.
No automated decisions. No decision about a household is made automatically. The
assessor reviews every figure, and the homeowner decides whether to proceed.
Transparency. Under clause 21 of the Terms, any change to the transmitted data has to be
disclosed to the user. The modified BER file is shown to the homeowner as "your home
with the agreed measures". The documents state that the energy uplift is an estimate
from the DEAP engine.

Consent management
No request goes to SEAI until both consents are signed and recorded. SEAI's checks on
the MPRN back this up.
1. Inform. At the survey, the assessor gives the homeowner a one-page privacy notice
(open item 3). It says what is requested from SEAI, why, what PlanitBER does with it,
who sees it, how long it is kept, and how to withdraw. This meets Article 13 and clause
3.5.
2. Two separate consents. The homeowner ticks and signs each one separately
(Schedule 1):
Consent A: "I consent to SEAI releasing the BER record for my home, MPRN
________, to John Turley (PlanitBER), BER assessor reg. 100615."
Consent B: "I consent to PlanitBER using that record, and a modified version sent to
SEAI's DEAP engine, to prepare my cost plan, pricing schedule and appendix."
Where an agent signs, a third line confirms their authority to act for the owner
(Criterion 3.2).
3. Verify. Before sending the request, the assessor checks that the name, address and
MPRN on the proof of ownership match the consent form. The MPRN must be clearly
legible, because SEAI withholds the datafile if it does not match (Criterion 3.6).
4. Record. Each consent record holds the date, the version of the wording, both
signatures, the MPRN and the request reference. It is kept in a consent log on the
assessor's encrypted computer.
5. Evidence to SEAI. The signed form and the redacted proof of ownership are uploaded
with the request, in the file format agreed with SEAI before acceptance (Criterion 3.5).
When SEAI asks for proof of consent (Criterion 3.3), the log entry and form are sent
within 5 working days.
6. Withdrawal. The homeowner can withdraw either consent at any time by phone or
email. Once Consent B is withdrawn, the datafile and modified file are deleted from the
assessor's computer. Documents already issued stay with the homeowner. The
household is left out of the next pilot return, and past aggregated returns cannot
identify them. Withdrawal is logged with its date.
The homeowner's rights
The assessor deals with every request within one month. All the data is held in one place,
so nothing has to be collected from other parties first. Where a request also concerns
SEAI's copy, the assessor passes it to SEAI and helps it respond (clause 3.7).

Risks and measures
Of the nine risks, two start at medium: a record pulled without valid consent, and a leaked
API key. With the measures below in place, all nine are low. Likelihood and severity are
judged for the homeowner, not the business.

Conclusion and sign-off
The residual risk is low and is accepted, so the processing can go ahead without prior
consultation under Article 36. It goes ahead only once the open items below are closed.
The DPIA will be redone, not amended, if PlanitBER becomes a hosted service or starts
making automated decisions about a household.
